# Empathy Map Canvas — ShopEEZ

To design an application that truly connects with its users, we map out the empathy profiles for our two target personas: **The Customer (Rahul)** and **The Store Administrator (Sarah)**.

---

## 👤 Persona 1: The Online Customer (Rahul)

### 1. What does Rahul THINK & FEEL?
- "I hope this website is secure and won't leak my credit card or password details."
- "I want to find products quickly without scrolling through endless unorganized listings."
- "I feel pleased when the checkout is quick and the UI is responsive."

### 2. What does Rahul SEE?
- A premium, dark-themed catalog interface showing clean product cards with prices, ratings, and brands.
- Interactive category tags that filter products instantly.
- A clear notification indicating whether a product is in stock or low-stock.

### 3. What does Rahul SAY & DO?
- **Says**: "I love websites that save my cart contents so I can think about my purchase and checkout later."
- **Does**: Uses the search bar, adds products to the wishlist, reviews cart items, inputs shipping addresses, and submits mock checkouts.

### 4. What does Rahul HEAR?
- Colleagues saying: "Try using ShopEEZ, it has a beautiful UI and is extremely easy to navigate."
- Critiques from tech-savvy peers: "Avoid sites that force you through a dozen pages just to buy one item."

### 5. Pains & Gains
- **Pains**: Slow catalog load times, lost cart selections, confusing checkout fields.
- **Gains**: Persistent cart storage, instantaneous client-side searching, direct checkout completion.

---

## 🛠️ Persona 2: The Store Administrator (Sarah)

### 1. What does Sarah THINK & FEEL?
- "I need a fast way to update product pricing and inventory without editing databases manually."
- "I want to check how many orders are pending fulfillment today."
- "I feel stressed when customers place orders for items that we actually ran out of."

### 2. What does Sarah SEE?
- An Admin Dashboard showing dashboard metrics (Total Sales, Orders, Registered Users).
- A table listing all orders with status values ("processing", "shipped", "delivered").
- Forms to quickly add a new product or edit an existing catalog item.

### 3. What does Sarah SAY & DO?
- **Says**: "Keeping the catalog fresh with updated images and stock levels is critical for our sales."
- **Does**: Monitors store logs, clicks 'edit' to adjust stock levels, changes order statuses, and deletes old accounts.

### 4. Pains & Gains
- **Pains**: Lack of high-level overview, complicated administrative consoles, delay in catalog synchronization.
- **Gains**: Real-time admin controls, direct database update hooks, simple order status management.
